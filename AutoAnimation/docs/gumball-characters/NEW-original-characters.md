# New Original Characters — Inspired by TAWOG Design Patterns

Based on analysis of 160 characters from The Amazing World of Gumball, these new characters follow the show's core formula: base object + minimal anthropomorphization + visual pun + contradictory personality + running gag.

---

## 1. Tempo — The Metronome

### Design Basis
An old wooden metronome with a swinging pendulum arm. Animated in a stop-motion style with visible wood grain texture.

### Appearance
Tempo is a small, upright wooden metronome with a triangular body tapering to a point at the top. His pendulum arm swings constantly from side to side, functioning as his single visible "limb" that he uses to gesture, point, and express. He has two small dot eyes near the top of his body and a thin line mouth. His feet are two small rubber pads at his base. A wind-up key protrudes from his back. The pendulum has a small brass weight that slides up and down — when he's anxious it slides to the top (fast ticking), when calm it drops to the bottom (slow ticking).

### Personality
Despite being literally built to keep time, Tempo is perpetually late to everything. He oversleeps, loses track of conversations, and spaces out mid-sentence. The irony drives other characters insane — they assume he'll be the organized one. He's actually a deeply creative daydreamer who gets lost in imagined symphonies in his head. His pendulum speeds up when he's nervous and slows down when he's bored, broadcasting his emotional state to everyone whether he likes it or not.

**Visual pun:** A metronome who can't keep time.
**Running gag:** His pendulum reveals his true feelings even when he's lying. Characters read him like a mood ring.
**Contradiction:** Built for precision, lives in chaos.

## 2. Vellum — The Vintage Map

### Design Basis
A folded parchment map with hand-drawn coastlines and compass roses. Animated in a watercolor/ink wash style — edges bleed softly, colors shift like wet paint.

### Appearance
Vellum is a rectangular piece of aged parchment, yellowed and slightly torn at the edges. Her face is formed by the compass rose in her center — the north arrow is her nose, the directional lines form eyebrows, and two small islands are her eyes. She has thin, tea-stained arms that fold and unfold from her creases. Her surface constantly shifts with faintly visible map features — mountains, rivers, and dotted trail lines that rearrange based on her mood. She wears no clothes; her "outfit" is whatever cartographic features are currently displaying on her body. When she blushes, her parchment turns pink like a sunset gradient.

### Personality
Vellum is a retired adventurer who has "been everywhere" but now works as the school librarian's assistant. She gives unsolicited directions to everyone, even for emotional journeys ("Your feelings are about three leagues northeast of acceptance"). She's wise and well-traveled but deeply lonely — maps are meant to be held, followed, and then put away. She fears obsolescence in a GPS world. Despite her worldliness, she has a terrible sense of direction in the actual town of Elmore and gets lost constantly.

**Visual pun:** A map who can't find her way around.
**Running gag:** Her surface displays a map to wherever the nearest character wants to go, but she can't control it — spoiling surprises and secrets.
**Contradiction:** Worldly and wise, yet lost and lonely.

## 3. Decibel (Deci) — The Hearing Aid

### Design Basis
A small behind-the-ear hearing aid. Animated in clean vector/flat design style — crisp lines, solid fills, no outlines, modern and minimal.

### Appearance
Deci is a tiny, curved beige hearing aid with a small speaker nub and a thin wire ear hook that curls above him like a single antenna. His face is on the flat outer surface — two small circular eyes and a rectangular mouth that lights up green when he's "on." He has two miniature stick arms and waddles on two nub-feet at his base. He's one of the smallest characters in Elmore, roughly the size of a thumb. A small volume wheel on his side involuntarily turns up and down based on his emotions. He occasionally emits feedback whine when overwhelmed.

### Personality
Deci hears EVERYTHING — whispered secrets, thoughts people mutter under their breath, sounds from three rooms away. He is the biggest gossip in Elmore, not out of malice but because he literally can't help overhearing. He desperately wants to mind his own business but physically cannot. He's cheerful, chatty, and well-meaning, but people avoid telling secrets near him. His deepest insecurity is that people only tolerate him for the information he provides, not for who he is. When he's emotionally hurt, his volume drops to near-silent, and nobody can hear HIM.

**Visual pun:** A hearing aid who hears too much. When hurt, nobody hears him.
**Running gag:** Characters whisper around him, but he hears it all anyway and blurts it out. His feedback whine interrupts serious moments.
**Contradiction:** Designed to help people hear, but his own voice is the one nobody listens to.

## 4. Kindle — The Match

### Design Basis
A red-tipped wooden matchstick. Animated in a warm, flickering style — her outline subtly wavers like heat distortion, and her color palette shifts between warm oranges and cool blues based on mood.

### Appearance
Kindle is a tall, thin wooden matchstick with a bright red phosphorus head that occasionally sparks when she's excited or angry. Her face is on the red tip — two bright eyes and a small determined mouth. Her wooden body is her torso, with thin stick arms branching from the sides and two simple feet at the base. She wears a small scarf (the matchbox striker strip wrapped around her neck). When she's passionate about something, her head ignites into a small controlled flame. When she's sad, the flame goes out and her tip turns black and charred, requiring her to "relight" her enthusiasm.

### Personality
Kindle is the school's self-appointed motivational speaker. She's intensely passionate about everything — recycling, homework, friendship, you name it. She gives fiery speeches at the drop of a hat. The problem? She burns out FAST. She'll champion a cause for exactly one day, then move on to the next thing with equal fervor, leaving half-finished projects everywhere. She's also terrified of water and rain (for obvious reasons), which makes her dramatic and shelter-seeking at the first sign of weather. Deep down, she fears that she's disposable — matches are used once and thrown away.

**Visual pun:** A match that burns bright but burns out. Literally disposable.
**Running gag:** She catches small things on fire accidentally when excited. Her head chars black after every passionate outburst and she has to "reset."
**Contradiction:** Motivational speaker who can't sustain her own motivation.

## 5. Stitch — The Embroidery Hoop

### Design Basis
A wooden embroidery hoop with a partially completed cross-stitch pattern inside. Animated in an actual embroidery/cross-stitch style — his movements look like thread being pulled through fabric, with visible X-shaped stitches forming his features.

### Appearance
Stitch is a circular wooden embroidery hoop with fabric stretched across his frame. His face is stitched onto the fabric in colorful thread — cross-stitch eyes (X-shaped), a chain-stitch smile, and French-knot freckles. His expression can only change slowly, as the stitches physically rearrange (you can see the thread moving through the fabric). He has two arms made of dangling embroidery floss and walks on two thread-spool feet. A needle is tucked behind his hoop like a pen behind an ear. His "clothing" is whatever pattern is currently being stitched on his fabric face — it changes episode to episode, reflecting his mood or interests.

### Personality
Stitch is the most patient character in Elmore. He speaks slowly, thinks carefully, and never rushes. He's a meticulous planner who maps out every action in a grid pattern before executing. Other characters find him infuriatingly slow — by the time he's finished thinking about a response, the conversation has moved on three topics. However, when others' plans fall apart, Stitch's slow-and-steady approach has already accounted for every variable. He's an exceptional listener and remembers every detail of every conversation (he literally stitches memories into his fabric). His greatest fear is unraveling — both literally and emotionally.

**Visual pun:** "A stitch in time saves nine" — he's always too slow but always thorough.
**Running gag:** His facial expression changes lag behind the conversation by several seconds. He finishes laughing at a joke long after everyone has moved on.
**Contradiction:** Maddening slowness that consistently produces better results than everyone's rushing.

## 6. Blackout — The Light Switch

### Design Basis
A standard white wall light switch plate with a toggle. Animated in a silhouette/shadow puppet style — when he's "off," he's rendered as a black silhouette with white eyes. When "on," he's fully lit and colorful.

### Appearance
Blackout is a rectangular white switch plate with a toggle switch as his nose. His eyes sit above the toggle and his mouth below it. He has thin arms extending from his sides and walks on two prong-like feet (like an electrical plug). His toggle switch flips involuntarily based on his mood — up (on) when happy, down (off) when sad. When "off," his entire body becomes a dark silhouette, and the area immediately around him dims. When "on," he glows warmly and brightens his surroundings. He wears no clothes — his plate IS his body.

### Personality
Blackout has severe mood swings that he cannot control. One moment he's the life of the party (literally illuminating the room), the next he's plunged into darkness and brought everyone's mood down with him. He's not depressed — he's bipolar in the most literal sense. He desperately wants consistency but his toggle has a mind of its own. Other characters sometimes physically flip his switch to cheer him up, which he finds deeply invasive ("You can't just FLIP someone's mood!"). He's introspective and philosophical about the nature of happiness, often asking whether being "on" is really better than being "off."

**Visual pun:** A light switch with mood swings who literally darkens the room.
**Running gag:** Characters flip his switch without permission. Important scenes go dark when he's nearby and gets sad.
**Contradiction:** Wants emotional stability but is literally a binary on/off mechanism.

## 7. Serif — The Typewriter Key

### Design Basis
A single vintage typewriter key (the letter Q). Animated in a woodcut/linocut print style — bold black lines, textured fills, slightly rough edges like a stamped impression.

### Appearance
Serif is a round, ivory-colored typewriter key cap with the letter "Q" embossed on his face (the Q's tail curls down as his perpetual smirk). His metal key lever extends down as his body/spine, and two thin wire arms branch from the lever's sides. He walks on the two forked prongs at the lever's base. He's small but has excellent posture (rigid metal spine). His ivory cap has slight yellowing and hairline cracks from age. When he speaks, a faint "clack" sound accompanies each word, and occasionally a tiny ink ribbon impression of his words appears briefly in the air beside him.

### Personality
Serif is a retired journalist who now writes the school newspaper's opinion column. He's eloquent, precise, and devastating with words — his insults are so beautifully phrased that people don't realize they've been insulted until hours later. He's obsessively particular about grammar and will interrupt conversations to correct people. He views the modern world (smartphones, texting, emojis) with contempt but secretly envies how easily modern devices communicate. His biggest secret: he can only type the letter Q. His sophisticated vocabulary is entirely verbal — anything he writes is just "QQQQQQQQQQ." He's terrified someone will find out.

**Visual pun:** A wordsmith who can only write one letter.
**Running gag:** He volunteers to write things, panics, and makes excuses. Occasionally someone finds a page of nothing but Q's and he blames it on a "artistic statement."
**Contradiction:** Master of language who is literally limited to a single character.

## 8. Gauge — The Tire Pressure Gauge

### Design Basis
A pen-shaped tire pressure gauge with a sliding measurement bar. Animated in standard 2D but with a metallic sheen effect — chrome highlights that shift as he moves.

### Appearance
Gauge is a cylindrical silver tube with a small rounded tip (his head) and a sliding white measurement bar that extends from his base (functioning as his single telescoping leg/body support). His face is on the rounded tip — two small eyes and a thin mouth. He has two small arms near his mid-section. The measurement bar extends and retracts involuntarily based on the "pressure" of social situations — sticking way out when stressed, retracting when relaxed. He wears a small rubber grip ring around his midsection like a belt. His chrome body reflects everything around him, so you can always see distorted reflections of other characters in his surface.

### Personality
Gauge is hyper-aware of social dynamics and tension. He can literally measure the "pressure" in any room and announces it involuntarily ("The social pressure in here is at 47 PSI and climbing"). He fancies himself a mediator and conflict resolver, constantly trying to "release the pressure" in tense situations. The problem is that by drawing attention to tension, he makes it worse. He's well-intentioned but socially catastrophic. He's also competitive about measurement — he'll argue with rulers about length, scales about weight, and thermometers about temperature. He considers himself the most accurate of all measuring instruments.

**Visual pun:** Measures pressure, creates more pressure.
**Running gag:** His measurement bar shoots out at the worst times, poking people. He announces tension levels during private moments.
**Contradiction:** A pressure release valve that increases pressure.

## 9. Mosaic — The Stained Glass Panel

### Design Basis
A rectangular stained glass church window panel. Animated in actual stained glass style — bold black lead lines separating colored glass segments, with light passing through creating colored shadows.

### Appearance
Mosaic is a tall, thin rectangle of stained glass with thick black leading lines dividing her into colorful segments. Her face is formed by the arrangement of glass pieces — two diamond-shaped amber eyes, a curved red piece for a smiling mouth, and triangular blue pieces forming eyebrow shapes. Different parts of her body are different colors (blue torso, green arms, red legs, purple hands), making her look like a walking patchwork. She has no consistent outline — she's defined entirely by the lead lines between her glass segments. When light hits her, she casts beautiful colored shadows on everything nearby. When she moves, you can hear a faint tinkling of glass.

### Personality
Mosaic sees beauty in everything and everyone, literally casting them in a better light (her colored shadows make everything look prettier). She's an artist and art teacher's pet who speaks in metaphors so elaborate that nobody understands her. She's fragile both physically and emotionally — a harsh word can crack her, and she has to be carefully repaired with new glass pieces, which sometimes don't match (leaving visible "scars" of different-colored replacement glass). Her greatest strength is that she's made of broken pieces arranged into something beautiful, and she tries to teach others that being broken doesn't mean being ruined. She has a pathological need to stand in sunlight and becomes dull and nearly invisible in shadow.

**Visual pun:** Made of broken pieces, creates beauty. Fragile but luminous.
**Running gag:** Characters accidentally crack her and have to find replacement glass. The repairs never quite match, making her increasingly colorful and mismatched over time.
**Contradiction:** The most beautiful character who is fundamentally broken. Needs light to exist but illuminates others.

## 10. Torque — The Skateboard Wheel

### Design Basis
A single polyurethane skateboard wheel with a bearing core. Animated in a graffiti/street art spray-paint style — slightly grainy texture, dripping paint edges, bold outlines.

### Appearance
Torque is a small, chunky polyurethane wheel — neon green with a visible metal bearing in his center (functioning as his single eye, like a cyclops). He has two short stick arms and no legs — he rolls everywhere using his wheel body, leaving faint urethane marks on the floor. He wears a tiny beanie on top and has a few sticker decals on his sides (band logos, skate brands). His bearing eye spins independently when he's thinking. He's heavily scuffed and flat-spotted on one side from hard riding, giving him a slight wobble when he rolls.

### Personality
Torque is a fiercely independent loner who refuses to be "part of the machine" — literally. He ran away from his skateboard (a complete board with three other wheels who keep trying to get him back). He sees himself as a free spirit, but everyone else sees a wheel without a purpose. He's fast, agile, and cool under pressure, but he struggles with the concept that independence doesn't mean isolation. He secretly misses his board-mates but is too proud to go back. He's into underground music, knows every back alley in Elmore, and serves as an unlikely guide to characters who are lost. His flat spot makes him thump rhythmically when he rolls, which he's turned into a signature beat.

**Visual pun:** A wheel who rolled away from his skateboard. Literally "spinning his wheels" going nowhere.
**Running gag:** His three former board-mates show up trying to convince him to come back. His flat-spot thump reveals his location when he's trying to hide.
**Contradiction:** Craves freedom but was literally designed to work as part of a team. Cool rebel who's essentially incomplete alone.
